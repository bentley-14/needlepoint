# Public-Disclosure Bar (31 U.S.C. § 3730(e)(4)) + Schindler + Original-Source

## Holding

31 U.S.C. § 3730(e)(4)(A) requires dismissal of a qui tam action if "substantially the
same allegations or transactions as alleged in the action... were publicly disclosed" in
one of several enumerated channels (federal hearings/reports/audits/investigations,
congressional/GAO reports, or news media), UNLESS the relator qualifies as an "original
source" under 3730(e)(4)(B): someone with knowledge INDEPENDENT of and MATERIALLY ADDING
TO the publicly disclosed allegations, who voluntarily disclosed that information to the
government before filing.

*Schindler Elevator Corp. v. United States ex rel. Kirk*, 563 U.S. 401 (2011), held that a
federal agency's written response to a FOIA request qualifies as a "report" for purposes
of 3730(e)(4)(A) — meaning that data or findings released via FOIA can itself constitute a
public disclosure, even though the relator (not the agency) obtained the FOIA response.

*U.S. ex rel. Customs Fraud Investigations, LLC v. Victaulic Co.*, 839 F.3d 242 (3d Cir.
2016), and *U.S. ex rel. Reed v. KeyPoint Gov't Solutions*, 923 F.3d 729 (10th Cir. 2019),
both hold that a relator's own **analysis** of publicly available records — data
compilation, cross-referencing, and statistical inference that produces a new inference
of fraud not evident from the raw records alone — can itself satisfy the "materially
adds" prong of original-source status, even where the underlying source data was public.

## Our application

DOJ's 2026 FOCUS Initiative explicitly frames data-mining analysis of public CMS/NPPES/
LEIE/court records as encouraged, non-disqualifying investigative work — consistent with
Victaulic/Reed. The correct posture is affirmative, not defensive: the relator's
contribution is the ANALYSIS (percentile extremity against the correct peer cohort,
cross-source ownership/reassignment mapping, convergent independent-signal synthesis),
which is exactly the "materially adds" knowledge Victaulic/Reed protect, not a mechanical
republication of public data.

**When to actually flag a public-disclosure concern** (and not before): a SPECIFIC prior
disclosure naming the SAME defendant and the SAME scheme —
- A prior qui tam complaint (its allegations remain a public disclosure event per
  3730(e)(4) even after dismissal — this is a DIFFERENT question from Carter's
  first-to-file pendency analysis; a dismissed prior suit can simultaneously (a) no
  longer first-to-file-bar under Carter, while (b) still counting as a public disclosure
  of the underlying allegations that this relator must show original-source status
  against).
- A DOJ/OIG press release, settlement, or CIA specifically naming this defendant.
- A news report on this defendant's specific conduct (not industry-wide reporting).
- Per Schindler, a FOIA-released agency report/investigation record naming this defendant.

Absent one of these SPECIFIC matches, do not pre-concede the bar; state the original-source
basis (independent analytical synthesis) affirmatively in the complaint.

## Common rebuttals / defense playbook

See frontmatter. The central defense move is arguing the underlying data sources are
public; the central rebuttal is Victaulic/Reed's protection of independent analytical
synthesis, plus insisting on the "SAME defendant, SAME scheme" specificity requirement
before conceding any disclosure is "substantially the same."
